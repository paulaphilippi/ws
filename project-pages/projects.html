Research Projects
Below is an overview of the research projects I lead or collaborate on across ambulatory stress assessment, evidence synthesis, and digital health.
READY — Reactivity of Affect in the Daily Lives of Youth
Lead PhD Project • University of Wuppertal
As my primary PhD project, I lead the READY study, which provides an ecologically valid framework for capturing and modeling short-term emotional regulation processes following everyday stressors in youth. Using a smartphone-based EMA design with a high-resolution microburst extension (assessing affect in fine-grained intervals directly after stressor occurrence), we examine how adolescents react to and recover from stressful events in real time (Minutes that matter). Beyond mapping these immediate affective trajectories and their situational moderators, we investigate how individual differences and real-time stress dynamics relate to longer-term mental health and well-being.

STRESSY — Stress Assessment in Young People’s Daily Lives
Co-Lead Project (Shared First Authorship) • Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis
I co-lead the STRESSY project together with Laura Meine (shared first authorship), in collaboration with Björn Schlier and Aleksandra Kaurin. In this systematic review and meta-analysis, we synthesize how stress is conceptualized, operationalized, and quantified in young people’s daily lives across EMA studies. We examine study designs, item semantics, stressor prevalence, and within-person variability, and meta-analytically evaluate associations between ambulatory stress indices and mental health outcomes to derive practical recommendations for future ambulatory research.

RehaCAT+ — Computer-Adaptive Routine Monitoring in Rehabilitation
BMBF-Funded Collaborative Project • Ulm University (PI: Prof. Dr. Harald Baumeister)
Prior to my move to Wuppertal, I worked on the RehaCAT+ project at Ulm University (funded by the Federal Ministry of Education and Research in cooperation with 12 rehabilitation clinics across Germany). We developed and evaluated a computer-adaptive patient-reported outcome monitoring system with integrated feedback and action recommendations, accompanied by research on technology acceptance (UTAUT) and digital health tools in routine care.
