About Me

I study how young people experience and regulate stress and emotions in their everyday lives — and how digital technologies can help us better capture, understand, and support mental health.

In my work, I combine smartphone-based Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA) and high-resolution microburst designs (READY study) with systematic reviews and meta-analyses on daily stress measurement (STRESSY), passive mobile sensing, and digital health interventions. Methodologically, I enjoy working with intensive longitudinal data, multilevel modeling, machine learning, and reproducible Open Science workflows.

Alongside my research at the University of Wuppertal, I teach B.Sc. seminars on psychological assessment and experimental psychopathology. Feel free to get in touch if you have questions about our work, want to exchange ideas, or are interested in collaborating!

Research Focus
Everyday Stress & Affect Dynamics
Capturing how youth respond to and recover from daily stressors in real time using high-resolution EMA microburst designs, examining situational and person-level moderators as well as links to mental health.
